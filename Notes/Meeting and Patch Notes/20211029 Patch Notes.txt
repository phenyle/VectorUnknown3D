>New Stuff
Dynamic Ship Locations:
Every play through will now generate a set number of ships (pending level) at random locations (within limits)
The new system now creates its own selections of vectors and matrixes to hit the ships
---Algorithm flow works like this---
-For each stage, the ships are randomly scattered across the field (and made to not overlap or be too close)
-For easy levels (level 1 and 2) the ships will only be placed in locations that are multiples of 5 eg [40,25], [-50,75], etc
-Each ship knows where it is so it knows what the answer is.  
-There are two preset lists of possible vectors choices, 
-easy list is things like [1,0], [0,-1], [1,1], etc
-hard list is things like [2,-1], [2,3], [3,0], [-1,3], etc
-Each ship (pending difficulty) chooses 4 vectors from the lists for its answers
-From there, each ship selects ONE of those vectors (ansV) as the actual answer vector (the rest are used, but herrings)
-From here the matricies are generated.
-First each ship finds the GCD of its location, ex GCD([40,50]) = 10, or GCD([15,45]) = 15
-for the lower levels the GCD is usually 5, 10, 15, etc.
-It uses this GCD and a random number to generate the first vector of the 2x2 matrix. 
-The random number range is determined as some integer between 0, and shipPosition / GCD
-we end up with two random numbers for X and Y (call them r1, r2)
-The first vector of the matrix is the product of the GCD * [r1, r2]
-this gives us [[a,c]...] of our matrix where a = GCD * r1 and c = GCD * r2
-from here using the chosen answer vector (ansV) from earlier we can determine [...,[b,d]]
-it follows that [b,d] = ([ship.x,ship.y] - ansV.x*[a,c]) / ansV.y
-for cases where ansV.y = 0, the algorithm throws away the [r1,r2] and finds a solution where ansV.x[a,c] = [x, y]
-for cases where the division results in a frational, the result is rounded and the ship position is adjusted
-in the case of rounding, this means there are edges where the ship location isn't exactly a multiple of 5
-after the answer matrix (ansM) is deteremined each ship generates 3 more herring matricies and adds them to their lists
Game Mechanic Wise:
The game picks a ship and random, asks it for its list of possible vectors and possible matrices and distributed them to the player interactable assets (the cannons and cannonballs).
Also, the game checks if there is another ship inbetween the cannon and the picked ship.
- if there is, the game changes the picked ship to be the one that's in the way first
- don't want to break the immersion of shooting through on ship to sink the one behind it, lol.
Addons:
Picking a vector and matrix will rotate the cannon to face in the direction of the resulting vector
- this sort of helpful in picking choices as you can see if the cannon is even facing the target
- the cannon does not rotate if the combination of ansV and ansM results in [0,0]
The targeted ship will have its goal location broadcasted above it and animated for better visual recognition
Cannonballs (the Matrix picker/signs) are much more noticable with glowing animated ring
